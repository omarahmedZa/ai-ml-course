Part of Topic 02 · Slope → gradient — the direction of a step

Equation lab · the direction of the step

Why the update subtracts

You can explain why w ← w − η∇f walks downhill, and why a minus sign does not mean the weights shrink.

Where this sits

w ← w − η∇f(w)

Lab 2 of 2 · the slope and the rate are dim; the subtraction is lit

∇f(w) is the gradient: the slope of the loss with respect to the weight. It points in the direction that makes the loss larger. Training wants the loss smaller, so it moves the other way — and that is the whole job of the minus sign.

The gradient points uphill

On f(w) = w² at w = 3 the slope is 6. A positive slope means: increase w, and the loss increases. To lose less, decrease w. The gradient told you the uphill direction; the update does the opposite.

Below the bottom the slope turns negative. At w = −2 the slope is −4, so decreasing w now increases the loss, and the same rule pushes w up instead.

Move μ and the whole bowl slides, floor and all. The dot is always the point where the slope is zero — the one place the update stops doing anything.

One step, with numbers

Take J(w) = (w − 2)² + 1 and start at w = 4. The slope there is 2(w − 2) = 4, and the loss is 5. With η = 0.1 the step is 0.4: w becomes 4 − 0.4 = 3.6 and the loss becomes 3.56.

Now add instead: 4 + 0.4 = 4.4, loss 6.76. Same size step, opposite direction, and the loss climbs. The sign is not decoration; it is the difference between training and anti-training.

Why subtracting is not shrinking

Every parameter in the model is a w, not just the weights on features. Take the canonical example: w = 2, b = 1, input 3, so the prediction is 7 against a truth of 10. The squared loss is 9.

The loss falls if the prediction rises, so the slope with respect to w is negative. Subtracting a negative adds: w ← 2 − 0.1(−18) = 3.8. Here the minus sign makes w bigger.

Check the last row

Slope 2(1 − 2) = −2, so the step is 0.1 × (−2) = −0.2. Subtracting it gives 1 + 0.2 = 1.2, with loss (1.2 − 2)² + 1 = 1.64. The weight grew and the loss fell, because the slope was negative.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"The minus sign makes the weights smaller." No. It makes the loss smaller, which sometimes means bigger weights. The gradient's sign decides which way w travels; the minus only guarantees you travel against it.

Correction: read the update as "opposite the slope, scaled by η", never as "subtract from w".

The tell: if every weight decreases on every step, the gradient can never be negative, and something is wrong with the derivative or its sign. A healthy run has weights moving both ways.

Retrieval check

  1. At w = 4 the slope of (w − 2)² + 1 is 4. Which way does the update move w, and why?
    Answer

    Down, to 3.6. The slope is positive, meaning w = 4 sits on the right wall of the bowl where increasing w raises the loss. Subtracting moves left, toward the bottom at 2.

  2. A weight has a negative gradient. Does it grow or shrink?
    Answer

    It grows: w − η(negative) = w + something. The negative slope says the loss falls as w rises, so the update obeys it.
