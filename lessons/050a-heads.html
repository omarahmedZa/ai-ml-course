h — one width, several views

You can read a head's weight row, say what a second head adds, and show that doubling h leaves the parameter count alone.

Where this sits

Lab 1 · only h is lit; Q, K and V are the projections each head owns

One head produces one weighted average of the values. Multi-head attention runs h of those in parallel, each with its own projections, concatenates the h outputs to width dmodel, and multiplies by WO. The outputs are not averaged; they are stacked and then mixed by one learned matrix.

A head is a row of weights

Take the four tokens the · cat · sat · down and a causal mask, so each row can only see itself and the tokens before it. Every row sums to 1. The two heads below have the same job description — blend the values — and completely different patterns.

Head 1 is a locality head: its weight halves with every position back, so down puts about half its mass on itself and a quarter on sat. Head 2 is an anchor head: 60% of every row after the first goes to the, and the other 40% to the token just before the query. Both rows for down sum to 1.0 and neither is more correct; they answer different questions about the same sentence.

The slider shows the family the table is one member of. A low r gives a head that sees only its own position and the one before it; an r near 1 gives a head that treats the whole prefix alike. Real heads sit somewhere between, and most are neither perfectly local nor perfectly global.

The same budget, split h ways

At dmodel = 512, each head's projections cost 4 × 512 × dhead. The four matrices are Q, K, V and the output mix inside the head, so the bill scales with dhead — and dhead shrinks as h grows.

The last column never moves: 4 × 512² = 1,048,576. Eight heads of 64 use exactly the columns one head of 512 would use. Multi-head is a factoring of the same budget, not a second budget, and that is why h can be changed without changing the model's size.

The trap— h

Most common wrong mental model

"More heads means more parameters, so more heads is more capacity." The parameter count is flat in h. What changes is the shape of the computation: more, narrower subspaces, each with its own softmax row.

Correction: treat h as a factoring choice. Too few heads gives one blended pattern; too many gives many tiny ones, some redundant and prunable. Neither is free capacity.

The tell: a parameter table that grows when h grows. If the total moved, the implementation is projecting to full width per head before splitting, and the model is h times larger than intended.

Retrieval check— h

  1. dmodel = 512 and h = 16. What is dhead, and what do all heads cost together?
    Answer

    dhead = 512/16 = 32. Each head costs 4 × 512 × 32 = 65,536, so sixteen heads cost 1,048,576 — identical to one head of 512. The width was divided, not duplicated.

  2. Head 1's row for down is 0.533, 0.267, 0.133, 0.067. What is the ratio between neighbours, and what kind of head is it?
    Answer

    Each weight is half the one closer to the query: r = 0.5. That is a locality head — it mostly uses its own token and the one just before it, and pays little attention to anything further back.
