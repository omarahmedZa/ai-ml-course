argmin vs min

You can say which one returns a position, which returns a height, and why training needs the position.

Five labs built the thing being minimised. This lab reads the operator in front of it. It answers one question: what exactly does training hand back?

Two questions, two answers

Take a bowl-shaped loss. You can ask how low it goes, or where it is lowest. They are different numbers, with different units.

Check the second row

argmin = −1, min = 0.5. The squared term is zero at θ = −1; the 2 scales the height, it does not move the position.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"argmin gives the smallest loss." That is min. argmin gives the input that produces the smallest loss. In this course the input is θ, so argmin hands you a set of weights, not a score.

Correction: read it as "argument of the minimum" — the argument is the thing you plug in.

The tell: check the units. A loss value is measured in points; θ is a weight. If your answer to "what is θ*?" has the same units as the loss, you reported the min by mistake.

Retrieval check

  1. J(θ) = (θ − 3)² + 4. What are argmin and min?
    Answer

    argmin = 3 (the position), min = 4 (the height). Two questions, two answers, different units.

  2. Model A has minimum loss 2 at θ = 5. Model B has minimum loss 3 at θ = 1. Which is better, and what is its θ*?
    Answer

    A, because 2 < 3. Its θ* is 5. You compare models by min, then deploy the winner's argmin.
