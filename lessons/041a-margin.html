Where this sits

Lab 1 of 1 · the margin is lit; the point's position m stays dim

A support vector machine does not just want a line that separates the classes. It wants the widest empty band it can get around that line. The half-width of the band is the margin, and every point is scored by how far inside that band it sits.

The cost of standing inside the band

Let m be a point's signed distance from the boundary: positive on its own side, negative on the wrong side. If the margin is 1, a point at m = 0.4 is inside the band by 0.6 and pays 0.6. A point at m = 1.6 has cleared the band and pays nothing.

The figure plots that price. Drag the margin and the whole line shifts: the point where the cost reaches zero is the edge of the band you are demanding.

Three points sit at m = 0.2, m = 0.9 and m = 1.6 with the margin at 1. Their costs are 0.8, 0.1 and 0. The first two are support vectors; the third is correctly classified and clear of the band, so moving it further out would not change the line at all.

Only the support vectors constrain the answer. That is the surprising economy of the SVM: a thousand rows may be stored, but the fit is decided by the handful sitting on or inside the band.

The width of the band is 2/‖w‖, so a boundary with small weights has a wide margin. Widening the margin pushes the boundary away from the nearest points, which is safer on new data — until the band starts swallowing points of the other class.

C is the price of that trade. The full objective is ½‖w‖² + C × Σ cost. A large C makes each violation expensive, so the model keeps the band narrow to avoid paying; a small C lets violations go cheaply, so the model widens the band and accepts more of them.

The trap— margin

Most common wrong mental model

"The margin is the gap between the two classes." The gap is where the classes happen to be. The margin is a band the model chooses, wide enough to be safe and narrow enough to avoid swallowing points.

Correction: the support vectors do not sit outside the margin; they sit on its edge or inside it, and they are the only rows that set its width.

The tell: if moving a distant, correctly classified point changes your boundary, the model is not behaving like an SVM. Only points with a non-zero cost should move it.

Retrieval check— margin

  1. With margin 1, a point sits at m = 0.4. What does it pay, and is it a support vector?
    Answer

    It pays 1 − 0.4 = 0.6. It is inside the band, so it is a support vector and its position helps set the boundary.

  2. What changes when C drops from 100 to 0.1?
    Answer

    Violations become cheap, so the model accepts a wider band and more mistakes inside it. The margin widens, more points become support vectors, and the fit is less sensitive to individual rows.
