Pretraining: prediction as supervision

You can contrast next-token and masked objectives, and explain what scale buys.

Why this matters

Pretraining is why an LLM needs no labels: every document is its own answer key. It also explains why the model is a probability distribution over text, not a database of facts.

The one core idea

Self-supervision: hide part of the text, predict it, and the gradient teaches everything needed to predict it. No human labels; the loss is just cross-entropy on tokens.

How it actually works

GPT-style (causal)BERT-style (masked)
PredictsNext token, left to rightMasked tokens from both sides
SeesOnly the pastThe whole context
Good atGenerationClassification, extraction
Trained onEvery position in the document~15% of positions
  • Loss: mean negative log-likelihood of the true next token. Same object as topic 3.
  • Perplexity = exp(loss) — "effectively choosing between this many equally likely tokens". Lower is better.
  • Scale: loss falls as a power law in parameters, data, and compute. Capability improvements are mostly predicted by that curve, not by architecture tweaks.
  • What is learned: a compressed model of text statistics — syntax, facts, style, some reasoning procedures. Not a lookup table, and not verified truth.
perplexity = exp( −(1/N) Σ log p(token_t | token_<t) )

Worked example

1. Reading a loss number

Loss (nats)PerplexityPlain reading
5.0148Barely better than random over a 50k vocab
3.020Roughly "one of 20 plausible next tokens"
2.07.4Strong local fluency

e³ ≈ 20.1. A drop of one nat is a 2.7× reduction in effective uncertainty — which is why leaderboard differences of 0.05 nats are meaningful, not noise.

2. Why masking differs from causal

Causal training gives a gradient at every position; masked training gives one at 15% of positions but with bidirectional context. That is why BERT is a stronger encoder and a useless generator: it never learned p(next | past).

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"A lower perplexity means a better assistant." Perplexity measures fit to the pretraining distribution. It says nothing about instruction following, factuality, or safety — those come from post-training (topic 29).

Second trap: treating benchmark scores as independent evidence. Public test sets leak into pretraining corpora; a score can reflect memorisation of the benchmark rather than the skill.

Step by step

If the mechanism above went past you, walk it one click at a time. Each step is one sentence and one idea.

Retrieval check

  1. A model reports loss 2.3 nats. What is its perplexity, and what does that mean?
    Answer

    e²·³ ≈ 10. At each position it is about as uncertain as a uniform choice among 10 tokens — reasonably fluent, far from solved.

  2. Why can a causal LM be used for generation but a masked LM cannot?
    Answer

    The causal model is trained on p(token | previous tokens), exactly the distribution needed to sample text. The masked model is trained to fill gaps using both sides, which is unavailable during left-to-right generation.

  3. What does self-supervision replace, and what does it cost?
    Answer

    It replaces human labels with the text itself. The cost is scale: you need enormous corpora and compute, and the objective optimises plausibility rather than truth.

Build it in Colab

Runnable, NumPy-only, no setup: open the notebook in Colab · local copy. The cell prints numbers, not pictures — change one value and rerun to see what breaks.

from collections import Counter, defaultdict
import numpy as np
text = ("the cat sat on the mat . the cat ate the fish . "
        "the dog sat on the mat . the dog ate the bone .").split()
bigram = defaultdict(Counter)
for a, b in zip(text, text[1:]):
    bigram[a][b] += 1
