Part of Topic 02 · Slope → gradient — the size of a step

Equation lab · the learning rate

η — how far one step goes

You can say what η controls, and tell a step that is too small from one that is too large.

Where this sits

w ← w − η ∇f(w)

Lab 1 of 2 · only η is lit; the slope and the weights stay dim

η (eta) is the learning rate. The gradient ∇f says which way the ground rises; η says how much of that reading you act on. Training repeats w ← w − η∇f(w) until the loss stops falling.

A step size, not a speed

Take f(w) = w², the bowl used all through topic 2. Its slope is 2w. At w = 3 the slope is 6, so a rate of η = 0.1 moves w by 0.6 and lands on 2.4. The loss drops from 9 to 5.76.

η is not a speed in time. It is a fraction of the slope, so the same η takes a big step where the ground is steep and a tiny step near the bottom, where the slope is small.

Three rates, three fates

Same start, w = 3. Same bowl, eight steps. Only η differs, and it decides whether the loss reaches the bottom or never moves at all.

Each step multiplies the distance to zero by |1 − 2η|: that is 0.9 at 0.05, 0.4 at 0.3, and exactly 1 at 1.0.

At η = 1.0 the factor is 1, so the distance never shrinks. The weight flips 3 → −3 → 3 and the loss sits at 9 forever. The step has the right direction and the wrong length.

Choosing η

A small η is not safer, only slower. At 0.05 the distance shrinks by 10% per step, so it takes roughly 40 steps to do what 0.3 does in five.

A large η overshoots the bottom and climbs the opposite wall. On this bowl the dividing line is η = 1: below it the loss falls, at it the loss is stuck, above it the loss grows without bound.

Check the last two rows

Row 3: 0.1 × 2 = 0.2, so w = 1 − 0.2 = 0.8. Row 4: the slope is −2, so the step is −0.2, and subtracting a negative moves w up: −1 + 0.2 = −0.8. On the left side of the bowl the same rule walks uphill toward zero.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"A bigger η means faster learning." Up to a point, yes. Past it, a bigger η means bigger steps that miss the bottom, and the loss gets worse while the weights swing wildly.

Correction: judge the rate by the loss curve, not by the step size. A converging run shows a falling loss and shrinking steps; a diverging run shows the same loss repeating or climbing.

The tell: the loss prints 9, 9, 9 while the weights alternate in sign. That is not slow learning; that is a step that lands exactly as far on the far side every time.

Retrieval check

  1. At w = 2 the slope of f(w) = w² is 4. With η = 0.1, what is the new w?
    Answer

    The step is 0.1 × 4 = 0.4, so w becomes 2 − 0.4 = 1.6. The loss falls from 4 to 2.56.

  2. Why does η = 1.0 leave the loss at 9 on this bowl?
    Answer

    The distance is multiplied by |1 − 2η| = 1 each step, so it is preserved and only the sign flips. The weight bounces between 3 and −3, and both give w² = 9.
