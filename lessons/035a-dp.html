ε and δ — the shape of a privacy promise

You can read the guarantee aloud, set clip C and noise σC, and say what the noise costs in accuracy.

Where this sits

Pr[ M(D) ∈ S ] ≤ eε Pr[ M(D′) ∈ S ] + δ

Lab 1 of 1 · the multiplier is lit; the data, the mechanism and δ are dimmed

D and D′ are two datasets that differ in exactly one person. M is whatever leaves the machine — an update, a sum, a trained model. The inequality says those two outputs look nearly the same, so the released number barely depends on any one person.

Reading the two symbols

ε (epsilon) caps the ratio between the two probabilities. ε of 0 means the outputs are statistically identical; larger ε allows a bigger difference and therefore a weaker promise. δ (delta) is the small probability that the cap fails at all — usually written as far smaller than 1/n.

SymbolSay itWhat it controls

δdeltaThe chance the bound does not hold
Cclip normThe largest an update is allowed to be

Clip first, then add noise

One record can move an update by an unbounded amount, so no noise level could cover it. Clipping to norm C caps how far any single record can push the result: the sensitivity is now C, a number you chose. Adding noise with standard deviation σC then hides that push.

Raise σ and each record hides better, so ε falls. Lower σ and the model learns the data more sharply, at the price of a larger ε. There is no setting that wins on both sides; the pair (ε, δ) is a price list, not a quality score.

Spend a budget

ε is spent per release, and releases add up. Under basic composition, running 100 rounds at ε = 0.05 each leaves you with a total ε of about 5.0. That is why the budget is fixed before training starts, and why a per-round ε quoted alone is not a guarantee.

Check the last two rows

40 × 0.05 = 2.0, so forty rounds exhausts the budget exactly. 100 × 0.01 = 1.0: smaller steps per round buy more rounds for the same total.

The trap— ε and δ

Most common wrong mental model

"ε is the probability that privacy leaks." It is a bound on how much the output distribution may shift when one person joins or leaves — a ratio of eε, not a percentage. ε = 1 lets one record change the chance of an output by a factor of about 2.7.

Correction: always read ε together with δ, with the composition rule, and with the sensitivity that C set. A number without those three is not a guarantee; it is a parameter of one step.

The tell: the claim quotes ε = 0.1 and stops. No δ, no round count, no accountant. Ask for the total after composition, and for the clip norm — the same ε over 500 rounds is a different promise from ε over 5.

Retrieval check— ε and δ

  1. With C = 1 and σ = 1.1, what is the noise standard deviation, and what happens to ε and accuracy as σ rises?
    Answer

    The noise has standard deviation 1.1, since σC = 1.1 × 1. Raising σ lowers ε and lowers accuracy: 0.92 at σ = 0.5 against 0.81 at σ = 4.0.

  2. Forty rounds at ε = 0.1 each. What total does basic composition give, and why does that matter?
    Answer

    4.0. The budget is spent by the run, not by the last round, so a per-round figure understates the exposure by the number of releases.
