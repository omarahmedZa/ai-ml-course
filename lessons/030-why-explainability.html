Unit 8 · Explainable AI · Topic 30 of 35

Why explainability exists

You can distinguish intrinsic vs post-hoc and local vs global, and name the accountability use case.

Why this matters

Your project will make a prediction somebody has to act on. "The model said so" is not an answer to a rejected loan applicant, a clinician, or an auditor — and in some jurisdictions it is not a legal answer either.

The one core idea

An explanation is an argument about why an output occurred, addressed to a specific stakeholder. Different stakeholders need different arguments — and most XAI failures are a mismatch between the method and the audience.

How it actually works

AxisOption AOption B
WhenIntrinsic: the model is interpretable by design (linear, small tree)Post-hoc: explain a trained black box afterwards
ScopeGlobal: how the model behaves overallLocal: why this one prediction
FormFeature attribution: which inputs matteredExample / counterfactual: nearest case, minimal change
  • Debugging: a surgeon's model keys on the hospital's scanner ID, not the lesion. Attribution finds it; accuracy never would.
  • Accountability: the applicant is owed the actual reason, not a plausible-sounding one. GDPR Art. 22 constrains purely automated decisions with significant effects.
  • Trust and adoption: a clinician will not act on a score they cannot sanity-check.
  • Safety: explanations expose spurious shortcuts before deployment, when they are cheap to fix.

Worked example

1. The same model, three audiences

A loan model rejects an application with p(default) = 0.31 against a 0.25 threshold.

AudienceUseful explanation
Applicant"Debt-to-income and two recent missed payments" — actionable, specific
Loan officerFeature contributions plus comparable approved cases
RegulatorGlobal validation: error rates by protected group, plus the decision rule

One explanation cannot serve all three. Shipping SHAP values to the applicant answers a question they did not ask.

2. Intrinsic vs post-hoc trade-off

A logistic regression is fully interpretable and may lose 3 AUC points to a gradient-boosted model. That trade is sometimes correct (regulated lending) and sometimes not (ad ranking). The mistake is refusing to make the trade explicitly.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"An explanation tells you the cause." Post-hoc methods describe the model's function, not the world's causal structure. "Income contributed 0.2" means the model's output would change if income changed — not that income causes repayment.

Second trap: explainability theatre — producing a heatmap because it looks rigorous. An explanation nobody can act on, or that is never validated (topic 32), is decoration with a compliance cost.

Retrieval check

  1. A decision tree of depth 4 is used for credit scoring. Is it intrinsic or post-hoc, and local or global?
    Answer

    Intrinsic — the model is the explanation. It supports both local (the path taken) and global (the whole tree) readings.

  2. Why is a global explanation insufficient for an individual rejection?
    Answer

    Global describes average behaviour. The applicant needs the reason for their output, which may be a rare interaction invisible in aggregate importance.

  3. What is the difference between explaining a model and explaining a prediction?
    Answer

    The model explanation characterises the function everywhere; the prediction explanation is a local claim about one input. Methods differ: global importance vs local attribution.

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
n = 500
income = rng.normal(50, 15, n)
debt = rng.normal(0.3, 0.15, n)
