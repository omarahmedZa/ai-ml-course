Attention, one symbol at a time · Lab 2

1/√dk — keeping scores in range

You can explain why scores are divided by the square root of the head width, and what a missing divisor does to learning.

Where this sits

Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax( QKT / √dk ) V

Lab 2 of 2 · every part is dimmed except the divisor

A score is a dot product: multiply matching positions of a query and a key, then add. With head width dk, that is dk products. This lab is about the one number that controls how large such a sum becomes.

A sum of d terms wanders

Each product is roughly ±1. They do not cancel neatly, so the sum drifts: its standard deviation grows like the square root of the count. Width 4 gives a spread near 2; width 64 gives 8; width 512 gives about 22.6. Nothing is wrong — a wider head simply adds more terms.

What a spread of 8 does

Take eight tokens whose scores are spread by about 8. The exponentials differ by thousands, so at width 64 the largest weight in the row is already 0.99; at width 512 it is 0.999 and every other token rounds to noise. Softmax has stopped sharing anything.

Divide by the square root

If each product has variance 1, a sum of d of them has variance d, so its spread is √d. Dividing by √d restores a spread of 1 at every width. Dividing by d over-corrects: at d = 64 a gap of 8 becomes 0.125, giving weights 0.531 and 0.469, and every head averages nearly everything.

Why saturation stops learning

Learning needs the slope of weight with respect to score, which is p(1 − p) for a weight p. At p = 0.9997 the slope is 0.0003; at p = 0.67 it is 0.22. Multiply a few hundred such slopes through the backward pass and early layers get nothing to update. In 32-bit floats, 1 − 0.9997 may even round to zero.

The trap— 1/√dk

Most common wrong mental model

"The √dk is a tuning constant — drop it, or replace it with dk." It is set by the width, not chosen by taste. Drop it and softmax saturates as width grows; use dk instead and softmax flattens as width grows. Both still run, which is why the bug survives a demo.

Correction: the divisor exists to keep the score spread near 1, so one softmax behaves the same at width 64 and width 512.

The tell: log the standard deviation of your score matrix at initialisation. It should be near 1. Near 8 at d = 64 means the divisor is missing; near 0.1 means it is too large. A second tell is a gradient norm at the first block that is orders of magnitude below the last.

Retrieval check— 1/√dk

  1. A head is 100 numbers wide. What divisor keeps the score spread near 1?
    Answer

    √100 = 10. The spread grows like the square root of the width, so the correction is the same root.

  2. Why does a one-hot attention row block learning?
    Answer

    Because the slope p(1 − p) goes to zero as p goes to 1. The layer still produces an output, but almost no gradient reaches the weights that made the scores.
