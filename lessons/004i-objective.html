The objective, one symbol at a time · Lab 9

The whole objective

You can read the full expression left to right and say what training does to the landscape.

Where this sits

θ* = argminθ (1/n) Σi L( fθ(xi), yi )

Each symbol has had its own lab. θ is every number the model learns — here the pair (w, b). xi is one fixed example, fθ(xi) the model's prediction for it, L the price of being wrong about yi, Σ the adding, 1/n the averaging, argmin the search, θ* the answer.

Read it left to right

Nine symbols, nine jobs. Read the table down and the expression stops being a wall.

SymbolIts one job
θName every number the model learns — here w and b.
xiPoint at one example: its inputs, fixed.
fθ(xi)Produce the prediction that the current θ gives for that example.
LPrice that prediction against the true answer yi.
the per-example termHold one example's whole story as a single number: prediction, then price.
ΣiAdd those numbers across all n examples.
1/nDivide the total by n, turning a sum into an average.
argminθAsk which θ makes that average smallest — and return the θ, not the value.
θ*Name the winner: the parameters you keep.

Try it as a sentence: pick the θ whose average price per example is lowest. That is the entire objective, spoken.

The landscape

With two parameters the objective has a shape you can draw. Put w on one axis, b on the other, and loss as height. Every ring in the figure is one loss value, so the centre — w = 2, b = 1 — is the bottom of the bowl.

What training does

Training never sees the whole landscape. It stands at one point, measures the local slope — the direction in which loss rises fastest — and takes a small step the other way. Then it measures again. The map is not read; it is felt, one point at a time.

The dashed path is one such walk: it starts high, near w = 0.6 and b = 2.7, and each step lands closer to the centre. Nothing in the step knows where θ* is. The walk simply keeps going downhill until the slope flattens.

Faded example

Fill the blanks: “Training finds the ? that makes the ? loss smallest.”

Check the blanks

θ, and average. Training returns parameters; the thing being minimised is the averaged loss, not the parameters.

Then the sharper question: which part of the expression makes it an average rather than a total?

Answer

The 1/n. Drop it and you are minimising a sum, which grows just by having more examples — so a bigger dataset would look worse.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

“Minimising this guarantees good predictions on new data.” No. It minimises the loss on the data you already have. Whether that carries to data you have not seen is a separate question, answered by a separate test set — Topic 05.

Correction: the objective describes fit, not foresight. It can be driven to zero by memorising.

The tell: training loss falls while test loss rises. The walk is still going downhill on the map it was given, and the map has stopped matching the world.

Retrieval check

  1. Which symbol turns a sum into a rate per example?
    Answer

    1/n. Ten examples with prices 2 each sum to 20, an average of 2. The average is comparable across dataset sizes; the sum is not.

  2. What does argmin return — a loss or a θ?
    Answer

    A θ. It answers “where is the bottom”, not “how low is it”. The height is min; the position is argmin, and the position is what you deploy.
