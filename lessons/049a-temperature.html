Part of Topic 49 · Decoding & sampling — where temperature and top-p are introduced

Decoding, one symbol at a time · Lab 1

T — one dial, two names

You can compute the top-token share at three temperatures, and say how many tokens top-p keeps at each one.

Where this sits

p = softmax( z / T )

Topic 49 · only T is lit; z is the vector of raw logits, one per candidate token

The last layer returns a logit per vocabulary item: unbounded, possibly negative, not required to sum to anything. Softmax exponentiates each one and divides by the total. Temperature divides every logit by the same number first, so T = 1 leaves the model exactly as trained, T below 1 widens the gaps, and T above 1 narrows them.

No token changes place. Only the shares move.

Temperature is a gap multiplier

Softmax sees z and T only through the ratio z/T. Doubling every logit does exactly what halving T does. With two candidates only the gap Δ = z1 − z2 matters, and the leader's share is 1/(1 + e−Δ/T) — a sigmoid in Δ whose steepness is 1/T.

The fixed logit pair sits at Δ = 2; the height there is the leader's share. At 1/T = 1 it is 0.881 — the number the top-p step below produces after keeping the best two.

What the gate keeps

Top-p sorts the row and keeps the shortest prefix whose total reaches p. Take logits [3, 1, 0, −1], the threshold p = 0.9, and three temperatures.

Same vector, same threshold, three different gates. At T = 0.5 the leader alone clears 0.9, so one token survives and the sampler is effectively greedy. At T = 2 the mass has spread, the running total climbs slowly, and the gate must reach a third token. Renormalize the survivors or they no longer sum to one: at T = 1 the kept pair becomes 0.831/0.943 = 0.881 and 0.112/0.943 = 0.119.

That renormalized 0.881 is the same number the figure shows at 1/T = 1 and Δ = 2. Truncation did not change the race between the top two; it removed the runners that could not win.

The trap— T

Most common wrong mental model

"Higher temperature makes the model more creative, so it gives better answers." Temperature adds no ideas. It flattens probability the model already assigned, which gives unlikely tokens more chances and the correct one fewer. On factual work that is a tax on accuracy, not a boost in imagination.

Correction: choose decoding by task — greedy or very low T for extraction and code, higher T only where variety is the point and a wrong answer is cheap.

The tell: the same prompt scored on different runs without a fixed seed; or an extraction task that succeeds four times in five at T = 1 and five times in five at T = 0. A second tell is a top-p setting that keeps one token on a confident step and thirty on a flat one — working as designed, and a sign that a fixed top-k would behave very differently.

Retrieval check— T

  1. Two logits are 2 and 0. At T = 0.5, what is the top token's probability?
    Answer

    Divide by 0.5, which doubles the logits to [4, 0]. Then e4 = 54.6 against 1, total 55.6, so the share is 0.982. The scaled gap doubled, and the leader's share rose from 0.881 to 0.982.

  2. Why does top-p 0.9 keep two tokens at T = 1 but three at T = 2?
    Answer

    Raising T flattens the row, so each prefix holds less of the total. The first two tokens reach 0.943 at T = 1 but only 0.792 at T = 2, so a third token is needed to pass 0.9. The gate adapts to the spread; the threshold never moved.
