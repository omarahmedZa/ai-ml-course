Attention, one symbol at a time · Lab 1

softmax — scores into weights

You can turn a row of attention scores into weights that are positive and add to one, and say what those weights do and do not mean.

Where this sits

Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax( QKT / √dk ) V

Lab 1 of 2 · the scores already exist; only their conversion into weights is lit

The previous step produced one score for every pair of tokens. A score is any real number: 3.2, 0, −1.4. Attention cannot use those numbers directly. It needs weights — positives that sum to 1 across the row.

Scores are not weights

A score of 3.2 means "more relevant than a score of 1.0". It does not mean 3.2 of anything. Used raw, a long row would produce a bigger output than a short one, and negative scores would subtract content. Weights fix both faults: each lies between 0 and 1, and the row totals exactly 1.

Exponentiate, then divide

Two moves. Raise e (about 2.718) to each score. This makes every number positive and keeps the order: a bigger score still gives a bigger result. Then divide each result by the row total. Softmax has no parameters — it is a fixed reshaping of numbers the layer already had.

What the weights then do

The weights are used at once: the output is 0.67 × v1 + 0.25 × v2 + 0.09 × v3, where each v is that token's value vector. The result is the same width as one value, because it is a weighted average of values rather than a longer list of them.

Two tokens, by hand

Raw scores 1 and 0 are divided by √2 first, giving 0.707 and 0. Exponentiating gives 2.028 and 1.000; the total is 3.028. Then 2.028 ÷ 3.028 = 0.67 and 1.000 ÷ 3.028 = 0.33. Add the same constant to both scores and nothing changes — only the gap decides the split.

Why subtract the maximum

Implementations subtract the row maximum before exponentiating. Scores 1, 0, −1 become 0, −1, −2; the exponentials become 1, 0.368, 0.135; the weights are identical. The shift is housekeeping: e1000 overflows a float, and the answer must not depend on how large the scores happen to be.

Check the two missing weights

2.718 ÷ 11.107 = 0.245; 1.000 ÷ 11.107 = 0.090. The three add to 1.000, which is the only thing the divisor guarantees.

The trap— softmax

Most common wrong mental model

"These weights are the model's confidence." They are relative shares inside one row, produced by whatever scale the scores happened to carry. Multiply the row 1, 0, −1 by three and the weights become 0.950, 0.047, 0.002. The tokens did not change; the units did.

Correction: a weight says how much of the output comes from that token, not how sure the model is about anything. Calibration is a separate question, taken up in topic 29.

The tell: rescale the queries by two and watch the weights sharpen while the ranking stays fixed. If "confidence" moves without new evidence, it was never confidence.

Retrieval check— softmax

  1. Every score in a row is 0. What are the weights?
    Answer

    Equal: 1/n each. Equal scores give equal exponentials, and dividing equal parts by their total always gives the uniform share.

  2. Why can no weight be exactly 1 when the scores are finite?
    Answer

    Because ez is positive for every finite z. Every token keeps some mass; only an infinite gap would push the others to zero.
