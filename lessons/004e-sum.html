Σi — add the atoms

You can read the sum as a running total and explain why order is irrelevant.

Where this sits

θ* = argminθ (1/n) Σi L( fθ(xi), yi )

Lab 5 of 9 · the whole expression is dimmed; only the sum is lit

Σi means "add up over every example". The i is a placeholder that visits row 1, then row 2, and keeps going until the rows run out. The subscript only says which row you are on. Each row contributes one loss, and the sum is what they all cost together.

Turn rows on and off

Every bar below is one example's loss. Untick one and the total drops by exactly that loss; tick it again and the same amount returns.

Switch off x4 and the total falls from 30 to 14 — a drop of 16, which is that bar's height. The other three bars never move, because a sum only counts what is present.

Nor is 30 a score out of anything. It is an amount, and it would keep climbing if a fifth row existed.

Order does not matter

Same four losses, two different orders:

9 + 4 + 1 + 16 = 30

16 + 1 + 4 + 9 = 30

Addition is commutative: the order you list the rows in has no effect on the result. A total is a property of the set of losses, not of their sequence — so you can sort them, reverse them, or shuffle them and still land on 30.

What changes with order is only the running total at each step: 9, 13, 14, 30 against 16, 17, 21, 30. The destination is the same.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"Σ means average." No. Σ is a plain sum: add the losses, stop, report the total. Nothing is divided by anything.

Correction: the average is the next symbol, 1/n, which divides the sum by the number of examples. Two symbols, two jobs — Σ adds, 1/n scales.

The tell: a total that grows when you add rows is a sum; an average stays in the same range. Add a fifth example with loss 4 and Σ climbs from 30 to 34, while the typical loss per row barely shifts.

Retrieval check

Two questions on the running total, from memory.

  1. What is Σi of the losses 9, 4, 1?
    Answer

    14. Three rows: 9 + 4 = 13, then 13 + 1 = 14. No division involved.

  2. A total stands at 30 and you remove the example with loss 16. What is the new total?
    Answer

    14. Removing a row subtracts exactly its loss, because a sum is built only from the rows that are present.
