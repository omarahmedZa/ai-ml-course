Optimization, one symbol at a time · Lab 1

β (beta) — the share of the last step

You can compute a velocity by hand, and say what β = 0, β = 0.9 and β = 0.99 each do to a run.

Where this sits

v ← β v + g     w ← w − η v

Lab 1 · only β is lit; g is the gradient and η is the step size

Momentum keeps a second list of numbers, the velocity v, the same shape as the weights. Each step it adds the new gradient to a fraction β of the old velocity, then moves the weights by η times that running total. Set β = 0 and the velocity is just today's gradient, so the rule collapses back to plain SGD.

So β answers one question: how much of yesterday still counts?

The average behind the arrow

Unroll the rule and the answer becomes literal. After k steps the velocity is gk + β gk−1 + β² gk−2 + …, so the gradient from k steps ago enters with weight βk relative to today's. At β = 0.9 the memory is about ten gradients long; at β = 0.99 about a hundred; at β = 0 one.

The weights form a geometric ladder, and the ladder is the whole mechanism. A direction that repeats accumulates; a direction that reverses loses to its own history. The weights halve every seven steps at β = 0.9.

Two patterns, two verdicts

A ravine is steep across and shallow along. Across it the gradient alternates sign: +2, −2, +2. Along it a steady +0.2 points downhill. Push both patterns through β = 0.9 with η = 0.1 and the two verdicts separate.

Momentum divides the steady part by 1 − β and multiplies the alternating part by (1 − β)/(1 + β). At 0.9 that is ten times up and about nineteen times down, from one rule. The zigzag across the ravine is cut to 5% of its raw size while the march along it speeds up tenfold.

The first step is untouched: v starts at zero, so v1 = g1. The two runs part company from the second step.

The trap— β

Most common wrong mental model

"β is a second learning rate." It is not a distance. η still sets how far one unit of velocity moves the weights. β changes what a unit means: the steady velocity is g/(1−β), so the steady step is ηg/(1−β).

Correction: treat η and β as a product. Raising β from 0.9 to 0.99 multiplies every sustained step by ten, so η has to come down by roughly ten to keep the same run.

The tell: the loss falls, then spikes and oscillates with a constant amplitude instead of settling — the weights are crossing the basin and climbing the far wall. A sudden tenfold step after "just adding momentum" is β doing its job, not bad data.

Retrieval check— β

  1. The gradient is 1 on every step, v starts at 0, β = 0.9. What is v after three steps, and how far does the third step of η = 0.1 move the weights?
    Answer

    v1 = 1, v2 = 0.9 + 1 = 1.9, v3 = 0.9(1.9) + 1 = 2.71. The third step is 0.1 × 2.71 = 0.271, not 0.1. The velocity is still climbing toward its ceiling of 1/(1 − 0.9) = 10.

  2. Why can β = 0.99 at the old η diverge while β = 0.9 was stable?
    Answer

    The sustained velocity is g/(1−β), so 0.99 turns a gradient of g into 100g. With η = 0.1 the step is 10g — ten times the step that was already near the limit. Lower η by about the same factor, or the ravine walls are crossed in one move.
