Embeddings: meaning as a vector

You can explain the distributional hypothesis and compute cosine similarity.

Why this matters

Embeddings are the interface between language and every model in this course. Retrieval, clustering, recommendation, and the first layer of every transformer all reduce to "compare two vectors".

The one core idea

Words that appear in similar contexts get similar vectors. Meaning becomes geometry: direction encodes what a word is like, and cosine similarity measures how alike two words are.

How it actually works

  • Distributional hypothesis (Firth, 1957): "you shall know a word by the company it keeps." Context is the supervision signal.
  • Embedding matrix E of shape V × d. Token ID i → row E[i]. A lookup, not a computation.
  • Training: Word2Vec predicts a word from its neighbours (skip-gram) or neighbours from a word (CBOW). Gradient descent moves vectors that help prediction closer together.
  • Similarity: cosine (a·b)/(‖a‖‖b‖). Length is arbitrary, direction is meaning — exactly the case from topic 1.
  • Static vs contextual: Word2Vec gives one vector per word. Transformers give one vector per occurrence, so "bank" differs in "river bank" and "bank loan".
a = [0.8, 0.6], b = [0.6, 0.8] → cos = 0.96 / (1 · 1) = 0.96

Worked example

1. Three words, one space

WordVectorcos vs "cat"
cat[0.8, 0.6]1.00
kitten[0.6, 0.8]0.96
car[0.6, −0.8]0.00

cat and kitten point almost the same way; car is perpendicular — no shared direction, no semantic overlap in this toy space. The absolute magnitudes are meaningless; only angles are.

2. Where analogy arithmetic comes from

king − man + woman ≈ queen works because the offset "male → female" is roughly a constant direction across many word pairs. It is a property of the geometry, not a reasoning step — and it fails for most pairs, especially rare ones.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"Each dimension has a meaning." It does not. Dimension 37 is not "royalty"; meaning is distributed across all dimensions, and individual axes are usually uninterpretable.

Second trap: comparing raw dot products instead of cosine. A frequent word can have a larger norm purely because it appeared more often in training, and it will then win every similarity query. Normalise.

Retrieval check

  1. Two embeddings have cosine similarity 0. What does that say, and what does it not say?
    Answer

    Their directions are orthogonal — no linear relationship in the learned space. It does not mean they are unrelated in meaning; the space is trained for prediction, not for truth.

  2. Why does a static embedding fail on the word "bank"?
    Answer

    It has one vector for every occurrence, so it must average the financial and river senses into a single blurry direction. Contextual models produce a different vector per sentence.

  3. Your retrieval system ranks long documents first regardless of query. What is the likely bug?
    Answer

    Using raw dot products instead of cosine. Long documents accumulate larger norms, so the score measures length as much as relevance.

Build it in Colab

Runnable, NumPy-only, no setup: open the notebook in Colab · local copy. The cell prints numbers, not pictures — change one value and rerun to see what breaks.

import numpy as np
E = {
    "cat": np.array([0.8, 0.6]),
    "kitten": np.array([0.6, 0.8]),
    "car": np.array([0.6, -0.8]),
    "dog": np.array([0.9, 0.7]),
}
