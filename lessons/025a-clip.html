Part of Topic 25 · Policy gradient at scale — where the clip is introduced

Policy gradient, one symbol at a time · Lab 1

r — the ratio, clipped to 1 ± 0.2

You can compute PPO's clipped objective for any ratio, and say which side of the band the clip protects.

Where this sits

maximise min( r·A , clip(r, 1−ε, 1+ε)·A )

Lab 1 of 2 · r = πnew / πold · A is the advantage · ε = 0.2

PPO changes the policy in small steps because on-policy data is used once. A step that is too large wastes the rollout that produced it, and the policy may not recover. The ratio measures the size of that step.

What the ratio measures

r = πnew(a|s) / πold(a|s). π (pi) is the probability the policy gives an action in a state. The old policy collected the data; the new policy is what the current weights would do.

r = 1 means nothing changed. r = 1.3 means the action is 30% more likely than when it was taken; r = 0.5 means half as likely. The ratio is per action, so one update moves thousands of ratios at once.

The clip

With ε = 0.2 the allowed band is 0.8 to 1.2. PPO maximises min(rA, clip(r)A). Inside the band the two terms are equal, so the ordinary policy gradient runs. Above it, for a positive advantage, the clipped term stops moving and the objective goes flat.

Take A = +1. At r = 1.3 the unclipped term is 1.3, the clipped term is 1.2, and the min is 1.2. At r = 1.5 it is still 1.2. A good action 50% more likely earns no more than one 20% more likely.

Why the reward side stops

For a bad action, A is negative. At r = 2.0 with A = −1, min(−2.0, −1.2) = −2.0, so making a bad action twice as likely is still punished in full. The min takes whichever term is worse for the policy, which is why the cap binds on good actions but not on bad ones.

Check the last two rows

Row 2: min(1.8, 2.4) = 1.8. The min keeps the unclipped term, so clipping does not rescue a good action whose probability shrank. Row 3: min(6.0, 3.6) = 3.6.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"The clip guarantees the policy moves by at most 20%." It feels right because 0.2 sits in the formula and the objective stops rising. But the clip bounds the incentive, not the distance. Several epochs on one batch compound small steps, momentum carries direction across batches, and shared layers drag unrelated outputs along.

Correction: measure the change you actually made — the mean ratio and the KL between old and new policies — rather than trusting the formula.

The tell: after an epoch, the mean ratio on the same batch has drifted past 1.2, or KL spikes while every per-action loss looks capped. A run that improves for one epoch and then decays within the same batch points at epochs or learning rate, not at clipping.

Retrieval check

  1. A = +2, r = 1.25, ε = 0.2. What is the objective?
    Answer

    min(2.5, 2.4) = 2.4. The ratio is past 1.2, so the clipped term wins.

  2. Why is r = 1 the reference point?
    Answer

    At r = 1 the new and old policies agree, so the objective equals A. The ratio measures drift from the policy that produced the data.
