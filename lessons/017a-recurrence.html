Sequence models, one symbol at a time · Lab 2

h_t — one state, rewritten

You can read the recurrence symbol by symbol, and say why a fixed-width state loses the distant past.

Where this sits

ht = tanh( Wh ht−1 + Wx xt + b )

Lab 2 · one line, one fixed-width vector, one step at a time

ht is the hidden state after step t: a list of numbers summarising everything read so far. xt is the current token's vector. Wh and Wx are learned weight matrices, b is a learned shift, and tanh squeezes every number into the range −1 to 1.

One state, rewritten

Think of one whiteboard, not a notebook. At each step the model reads the board and the new token, wipes the board, and writes a fresh state. The board is 512 numbers wide at step 1 and still 512 at step 1,000. Width never grows, so the past survives only as a summary.

What each symbol does

Read the formula as three moves. Wx xt turns the current token into 512 numbers. Wh ht−1 mixes in the previous state. b shifts the result. tanh then squashes every number into (−1, 1), so the state cannot blow up.

Same weights at every step

Step 1 and step 1,000 share Wh, Wx and b. That sharing is why a model trained on 40-token sentences can accept 400-token ones. A separate matrix per position would need 1,000 × 416,256 weights for a 1,000-step sequence, and sequence length would become part of the architecture.

Why the signal fades

Training walks backwards through every step, multiplying one factor per hop. If a typical factor is 0.9, twenty hops leave 0.9²⁰ ≈ 0.12 of the signal and a hundred leave 0.000027. Nothing vanished at one step; the product did it, which is why the failure is invisible in a print statement.

The bars and the product measure different things. ht is bounded by tanh and can sit near 1 forever. The gradient is a product, and a product below 1 has no ceiling to save it.

Faded example

Check the last two rows

Row 3 is tanh(1.94) = 0.96. Row 4 adds 1 to that: tanh(1.96) = 0.96. The state is saturating — tanh is squeezing the added input, so more steps buy almost nothing.

The trap

Most common wrong mental model

"The RNN forgets because the hidden state fills up." It feels right because the state is called memory.

Correction: the state is fixed-width and overwritten, not filled. Nothing runs out of slots. The sharper limit is the training signal: a product of factors below 1 decays, so early tokens stop influencing the loss and their weights stop learning.

The tell: print the size of ht and the size of the gradient reaching step 1. The state stays near 1 while the gradient collapses. If the state looked healthy and the model still cannot use an early cue, the problem is the product, not the storage.

Retrieval check

  1. ht is 512 numbers at step 1. How many is it at step 500?
    Answer

    Still 512. Each step writes over the same vector; the width is a property of the layer, not of the sequence.

  2. What does 0.9²⁰ ≈ 0.12 describe — the state, or the gradient?
    Answer

    The gradient, or any signal passed back through twenty multiplications. About 88% is lost. ht itself is bounded by tanh and does not shrink this way.
