Symbols — how to read the maths

You can say every symbol out loud, and you know what it does before it appears in a formula.

Why this page exists— the reading key

A formula is not harder than a sentence; it is a sentence written in shorthand nobody taught you. The obstacle is rarely the idea — it is that ∂ has a name and nobody said it out loud. This page is the key. Every symbol is also named in parentheses the first time it appears on a lesson or lab page, so any page can be read on its own.

How to read a formula aloud— the grammar

Read left to right and give every symbol its word. θ ← θ − η∇L is "theta is replaced by theta minus eta times nabla L": a number, a step length, and a slope. ∂L/∂w is "partial L partial w" — how the loss changes when only w moves.

Two conventions are worth saying out loud once. A subscript means "of" or "at": fθ(x) is "f of x, with theta". A superscript star means "the best one": θ* is "theta star".
