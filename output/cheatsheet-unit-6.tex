\sect{Why tables die}
$|S|\times|A|$: grid 25, chess $\sim10^{44}$, Atari $256^{100800}$

\sect{Deadly triad}
function approximation $+$ bootstrapping $+$ off-policy $\Rightarrow$ divergence\\
Any two are safe. All three is where DQN lives.

\sect{DQN}
Replay: sample random minibatches $\Rightarrow$ breaks temporal correlation.\\
Target net: $\theta^-\leftarrow\theta$ every $\sim10^4$ steps $\Rightarrow$ fixed target.\\
$L=\big(r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\theta^-}(s',a')-Q_\theta(s,a)\big)^2$ (Huber)\\
Double DQN: select with $\theta$, evaluate with $\theta^-$.

\sect{Policy gradient}
$\nabla J=E[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,A(s,a)]$\\
Baseline $b(s)$: unbiased, lower variance; $A=Q-V$\\
PPO: $\min(rA,\ \mathrm{clip}(r,1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)A)$, $\varepsilon=0.2$\\
$r=\pi_{new}/\pi_{old}$; clip bounds the objective, not the weights.

\sect{Gotchas}
\begin{itemize}
\item Replay $+$ on-policy method $=$ invalid gradient.
\item PPO is sample-inefficient by design (on-policy).
\item Overestimation comes from $\max$ over noisy $Q$; Double reduces, not removes.
\end{itemize}
